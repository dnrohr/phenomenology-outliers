# DF: unable to judge shape consciously while the hand scales to grasp it

## Account

Following carbon-monoxide poisoning and hypoxic brain injury, DF developed profound visual-form agnosia. She was severely impaired at consciously discriminating an object's shape, reflectance, and orientation even though visual acuity, color vision, tactile recognition, general intelligence, and motor coordination were largely preserved.

Her action system nevertheless used visual geometry. When asked to indicate an object's width with finger and thumb, her estimate could be at chance; when actually reaching to pick it up, her grip aperture scaled to the same width. Likewise, she could orient her hand appropriately to post a card into a slot despite poor explicit orientation matching. Subsequent work found that grip scaling remained relatively preserved even when visual feedback during the movement was removed.

The original report also found partial whole-word reading despite difficulty with simple shape and orientation judgments, another content-sensitive island of performance.

## Circumstances and course

DF nearly asphyxiated in a shower shortly after turning 34 because of an improperly vented water heater. Initial near-blindness improved, leaving a stable and extensively studied form-perception deficit.

## Structure of the experience and performance

- **Conscious form report:** profoundly unreliable.
- **Color and texture:** comparatively preserved.
- **Touch-based recognition:** preserved.
- **Visually guided grasping:** uses size, orientation, and some shape information.
- **Feedback dependence:** the preserved scaling is not explained solely by online vision or touch.
- **Word recognition:** some whole-word identity can emerge despite elementary form difficulties.

## Key contrasts

`cannot report object width` / `hand opens to object width`  
`cannot match slot angle` / `hand rotates for posting`  
`simple form discrimination poor` / `some whole-word reading possible`

## Why this case matters

DF makes the inverse problem concrete: behavior reveals visually guided information that is not available in the form required for conscious perceptual judgment. “Seeing” must be divided by output and use, not treated as a single representation.

## Source boundaries

Later reanalyses debate whether DF's visuomotor performance is fully normal; the registry records the robust relative dissociation without treating it as an absolute all-or-none double dissociation.
