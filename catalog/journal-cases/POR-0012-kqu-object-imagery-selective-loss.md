# KQu: object shape and color imagery lost while faces, letters, and topology remain

## Account

After a left posterior cerebral artery infarction, KQu had severely impaired imagery for the shapes and colors of ordinary objects. Imagery for faces, letters, and topological relationships was preserved.

The dissociation was finer than “can/cannot visualize.” For object color, imagery failure accompanied color agnosia and color anomia: performance was equally impaired when questions required imagining an object's typical color and when choosing between correctly and incorrectly colored visible objects. For object shape, KQu retained more knowledge than could be generated as imagery: the patient could identify objects and reject incorrectly drawn pictures better than they could answer questions requiring an internally produced shape.

## Structure of the experience and performance

- **Object shape imagery:** severely defective.
- **Object color imagery:** severely defective.
- **Face imagery:** preserved.
- **Letter imagery:** preserved.
- **Topological/spatial-relation imagery:** preserved.
- **Visible object recognition:** better than internally generated object-shape access.
- **Color knowledge/access:** color imagery, naming, and color-validity judgment declined together.

## Key contrasts

`face image present` / `ordinary object image impaired`  
`letter and topology image present` / `object shape/color image impaired`  
`recognition of object form` / `generation of object form`

## Why this case matters

This case closely anticipates the survey result that inspired the outlier project: face imagery and generic object imagery can separate. It also shows that the boundary can differ for shape and color depending on whether knowledge remains accessible through perception.
