# LM: color and form remain while movement becomes discontinuous or frozen

## Account

LM developed a highly selective loss of visual motion perception after bilateral lesions in visual-association cortex with primary visual cortex spared. Stationary objects, shapes, colors, locations, distances, and depth could remain available, but motion lost its normal continuity.

In the classic functional descriptions associated with her case, pouring liquid was difficult because its surface could appear frozen and then suddenly be at a new level. People and vehicles changed position without a visible trajectory. Speech-reading from lip movement was impaired. Writing became easier with eyes closed because the seen movement of hand and pencil interfered.

Slow movement was more accessible than faster movement, so the deficit was graded rather than a universal disappearance of every displacement cue. Reaching and navigation became difficult because action normally depends on continuously updated motion.

## Structure of the experience and performance

- **Static form and object perception:** substantially preserved.
- **Color:** preserved.
- **Motion continuity/direction:** profoundly impaired.
- **Speed dependence:** very slow motion more detectable.
- **Cross-time inference:** new positions can be seen without the movement joining them.
- **Action consequences:** pouring, crossing streets, grasping moving items, writing, and reading lips become hazardous or effortful.

## Key contrasts

`object visible at successive positions` / `trajectory absent`  
`form/color/depth present` / `movement vision impaired`  
`writing with visual feedback difficult` / `writing with eyes closed easier`

## Why this case matters

LM demonstrates that visual experience is assembled from separable attributes. It also shows how removing a normally helpful sensory channel can improve action when that channel delivers unstable information.
