# EP: lifelong face recognition and face imagery deficit with normal object imagery

## Account

EP is a developmental case with no overt structural abnormality on MRI. He was markedly impaired at recognizing famous and highly familiar faces and at describing visual images of famous faces. Learning and later matching unfamiliar faces were also poor, especially for chimeric faces.

The impairment was selective rather than a general failure of vision or imagery. EP could judge age, sex, and expression from faces, identify facial parts, and distinguish faces from nonfaces. Basic vision and visual memory were intact. Object recognition from unusual viewpoints, gestalt completion for objects, within-category object discrimination, and visual imagery for objects were normal.

## Structure of the experience and performance

- **Familiar identity recognition:** impaired.
- **Face image description:** impaired.
- **Face-feature/category judgments:** age, sex, expression, parts, and face/nonface decisions preserved.
- **New-face encoding:** impaired, especially configural/chimeric processing.
- **Object imagery and recognition:** preserved, including difficult viewpoints.
- **Configural processing:** disrupted for faces but not houses/objects in the reported tests.

## Key contrasts

`object imagery present` / `face imagery impaired`  
`face parts and attributes available` / `face identity/configuration impaired`

## Why this case matters

EP supplies a complementary pattern to KQu (POR-0012). Together they argue that face and object imagery are not obligatorily yoked and that facial parts, attributes, configuration, familiarity, and identity require separate probes.

## Source boundaries

The original abstract reports test outcomes but little first-person language. This is an outlier account grounded mainly in structured behavior rather than a rich introspective narrative.
