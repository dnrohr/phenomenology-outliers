# MX: acquired loss of visual experience with preserved imagery-task performance

## Account

MX abruptly lost the phenomenal ability to generate visual images. Standard imagery questionnaires placed his subjective experience near the floor, yet he continued to perform normally on standard tests of visual perception, visual memory, and tasks conventionally assumed to require imagery.

The investigators used mental rotation and Brooks matrices tasks to test the mismatch. MX's success suggested that the correct spatial answer can be reached without a conscious visual picture, plausibly by a strategy different from that used by controls.

## Circumstances and neural findings

The paper describes an acquired change rather than a lifelong trait. During actual viewing of famous faces, MX's fMRI activation did not differ significantly from controls and included expected fusiform activity. During attempted imagery, however, he showed reduced activity across posterior regions and increased frontal activity relative to controls.

## Structure of the experience

- **Before/after contrast:** a previously available conscious capacity was abruptly lost.
- **Attempted voluntary imagery:** almost no phenomenal picture.
- **Perception:** famous-face viewing and ordinary visual perception remained available.
- **Task performance:** visual-memory, rotation, and matrix performance could remain normal.
- **Strategy:** the altered activation pattern was interpreted as evidence for a nonstandard solution route.

## Key contrasts

`successful visuospatial computation` / `visual phenomenology absent`  
`face perception network response preserved` / `imagery-related posterior response reduced`

## Why this case matters

MX is a direct warning against inferring conscious format from behavioral accuracy. The survey's experience/knowledge/inference distinction is essential precisely because an imagery-labeled task may be solved without imagery.
