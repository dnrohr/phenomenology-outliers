# External danger lacks fear but internal suffocation triggers panic

## Account

S.M., who ordinarily showed little fear of external threats, experienced intense panic during carbon-dioxide inhalation. An internal respiratory signal accessed fear by a route that bypassed the damaged system used for external danger.

## Why this case matters

This report constrains the catalog's taxonomy by separating capacities that ordinary experience tends to bundle together.
