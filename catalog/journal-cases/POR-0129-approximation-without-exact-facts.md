# Approximate answers remain when simple exact calculation fails

## Account

N.A.U. could judge numerical magnitude and generate approximate solutions despite being unable to perform even elementary exact calculations reliably. The case suggests a conscious sense of quantitative plausibility that does not depend on access to rote arithmetic facts.

## Why this case matters

This report constrains the catalog's taxonomy by separating capacities that ordinary experience tends to bundle together.
