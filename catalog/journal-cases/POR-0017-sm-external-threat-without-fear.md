# SM: external threats evoke curiosity without ordinary fear

## Account

SM, a 44-year-old woman with focal bilateral amygdala damage, showed a profound reduction in fear induction and conscious fear across questionnaires, daily-life sampling, films, a haunted house, and encounters with snakes and spiders. Intelligence, memory, language, perception, and emotions other than fear were broadly available.

Although she had long said she hated and avoided snakes and spiders, in an exotic-pet store she approached them with fascination, held and explored a snake, touched its scales and tongue, and repeatedly asked to touch larger dangerous snakes despite warnings. Her fear rating never exceeded 2/10. When asked why, she described overwhelming curiosity.

In a haunted house she showed excitement, laughter, approach, and attempts to scare performers rather than sustained fear. Scary film clips failed to elicit fear even though clips reliably produced other emotions.

## Everyday emotional life

Across 156 random prompts over three months, “afraid,” “nervous,” “scared,” and “fearful” remained at floor, alongside guilt and shame, while other positive and negative emotions varied. “Fearless” had the highest average rating. SM had survived gunpoint and knifepoint robbery, assault, domestic violence, and death threats. She recalled anger and upset but denied fear; her behavior often lacked urgency or avoidance.

## Structure of the experience

- **External threat perception:** dangers can be identified conceptually.
- **Fear feeling:** near absent across exteroceptive probes.
- **Action tendency:** avoidance is reduced and exploratory approach increased.
- **Other emotions:** not globally flattened.
- **Curiosity/arousal:** threat stimuli remain salient enough to capture attention and invite exploration.

Later work (not the primary source linked above) found that carbon-dioxide inhalation could still provoke intense fear/panic in SM and other people with bilateral amygdala damage. That follow-up suggests a further contrast between externally triggered threat and internal suffocation signals.

## Key contrasts

`knows an animal is dangerous` / `does not feel or enact ordinary fear`  
`external threat fear reduced` / `other emotions present`  
`avoidance absent` / `curiosity and approach amplified`

## Why this case matters

SM separates danger knowledge, fear feeling, arousal, attentional salience, and avoidance behavior. A single rating of “fearfulness” would miss much of the structure.

## Source boundaries

The paper's conclusions concern one rare lesion case. The later CO2 result is contextualized but not merged into the primary study's evidence.
