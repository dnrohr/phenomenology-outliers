# AJ: nonstop, automatic access to dated autobiographical memory

## Account

AJ's autobiographical remembering is described as nonstop, uncontrollable, and automatic. Given a date, she can report what she was doing and identify its day of the week with substantial accuracy and reliability. She spends excessive time recalling her personal past; memory is not merely available on request but intrudes on and organizes ongoing life.

The profile differs from expert mnemonists who deliberately encode arbitrary information with rehearsed techniques. AJ's exceptional access is selective for her own past and public events connected to it, rather than a general ability to memorize any material.

## Structure of the experience

- **Cue:** dates provide unusually powerful access points.
- **Retrieval:** rapid and automatic rather than constructed by an explicit mnemonic.
- **Control:** remembering is difficult to stop.
- **Content:** personal experience dominates.
- **Temporal metadata:** weekday and calendar date are tightly connected to event memory.
- **Burden:** the quantity and involuntary recurrence of memory consumes time and attention.

## Key contrasts

`personal dated events exceptional` / `arbitrary rote memory not equivalently exceptional`  
`retrieval accuracy high` / `retrieval control low`

## Why this case matters

AJ is a useful opposite pole to SDAM accounts, but not simply “more memory.” The dimensions include content selectivity, temporal indexing, automaticity, control, and burden.
