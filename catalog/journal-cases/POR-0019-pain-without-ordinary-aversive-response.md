# Six cases of pain sensation without ordinary withdrawal or distress

## Account

Six patients developed pain asymbolia after unilateral brain damage—five ischemic lesions and one traumatic hematoma. Primary sensation was not absent. They could register noxious stimulation, yet showed little or no withdrawal and absent or inadequate emotional responses when painful stimuli were applied across the body. Threatening gestures were similarly met without the usual defensive response.

The syndrome therefore does not reduce cleanly to “cannot feel pain.” The sensory event remains distinguishable while its ordinary unpleasant significance and action tendency are diminished.

## Structure of the experience and behavior

- **Noxious sensory detection:** preserved enough to distinguish stimulation.
- **Unpleasantness/distress:** markedly reduced or behaviorally inadequate.
- **Withdrawal:** absent or reduced.
- **Threat response:** similarly abnormal for gestures predicting harm.
- **Distribution:** responses were altered across the body rather than only in a numb region.

## Key contrasts

`pain-like sensation present` / `ordinary suffering reduced`  
`noxious event detected` / `withdrawal and defense absent`

## Why this case matters

The cases force “pain” to decompose into sensory quality, location/intensity, unpleasantness, motivation, and defensive action. This same decomposition is useful for ordinary interoception and emotion surveys.

## Source boundaries

The original abstract emphasizes behavior and anatomy more than direct first-person wording. Later philosophical shorthand such as “pain that does not hurt” should not be substituted for the patients' more complex profiles.
