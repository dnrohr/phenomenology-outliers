# Skills and new learning survive loss of personal past

## Account

M.L. lost access to large portions of personal identity and history while language, practiced skills, and the ability to acquire some new information remained. The gap was autobiographical rather than a uniform erasure of memory.

## Why this case matters

This report constrains the catalog's taxonomy by separating capacities that ordinary experience tends to bundle together.
