# Silent reading and thought with any voluntary voice but no default voice

## Account

The commenter says reading is experienced as though spoken, yet there is no voice: comprehension seems to jump directly to the understood meaning. Their own thought is similarly described as a continuous “silent cacophony.”

Despite the lack of a default or self-like inner voice, the person can voluntarily assign an imagined voice; while writing the comment they instantiate something like a poor Sean Connery impression. The unusual dissociation is therefore between spontaneous voice phenomenology and deliberate voice generation, not between auditory imagination present versus absent.

## Structure of the experience

- **Silent reading:** speech-like organization without heard voice.
- **Ordinary thought:** busy but nonsounding.
- **Deliberate auditory generation:** selectable voices are possible.
- **Self voice:** no neutral or default voice, including no stable copy of the speaker's own voice.

## Key contrasts

`speech-like understanding` / `auditory quality absent`  
`voluntary character voice present` / `default inner voice absent`

## Why this case matters

It separates verbal format, auditory vividness, voice identity, automaticity, and control—dimensions that a yes/no question about “having an inner monologue” would conflate.
