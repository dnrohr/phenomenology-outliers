# Observed impacts produce body-part-specific pain and pleasant touch

## Account

The commenter reports literal bodily sensations when observing or even imagining events affecting someone else. The strength varies greatly by body part. Seeing someone at risk of falling can produce unpleasant tingling in the commenter's feet; a film character landing feet-first after a fall produced a sudden pain up the shins intense enough to nearly provoke a scream in a crowded theater.

Graphic cuts and injuries can provoke a brief whole-body reaction. When the commenter's girlfriend cut her thumb, helping re-dress the wound was difficult because the observed injury hurt the commenter too. At lower intensity the phenomenon can feel less like current pain and more like a strong sensory memory accompanied by automatic flinching.

The same mapping is not exclusively aversive. Watching somebody receive a comforting back rub produces warm tingling in the corresponding area, compared by the commenter to ASMR.

## Structure of the experience

- **Triggers:** direct observation, film, anticipated falls, and sometimes mere imagination.
- **Somatotopy:** feet and shins are especially vivid; other injuries may produce localized or whole-body reactions.
- **Intensity:** ranges from tingling or remembered sensation to acute, near-crying pain.
- **Valence:** both painful and pleasant observed touch can transfer.
- **Duration:** strong injury responses are described as lasting a second or two.

## Functional consequences

The commenter avoids looking at severe screen injuries and believes the response would prevent work in a medical setting because it interferes with focusing on helping.

## Key contrasts

`observed event` / `felt bodily location`  
`painful observed touch → pain` / `comforting observed touch → warmth/tingling`

## Why this case matters

It exposes separable dimensions within social perception: knowledge that another body is affected, automatic mapping to one's own body, sensory quality, intensity, and aversive or pleasant value.
