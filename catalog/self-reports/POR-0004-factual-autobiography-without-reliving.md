# A fact-rich autobiography without first-person reliving or remembered emotion

## Account

The poster reports remembering their life as facts and bullet points. They cannot re-experience events and do not remember their past emotions, yet before encountering the concept of SDAM they did not regard their everyday memory as worse than other people's. They describe only modest ordinary failures such as forgetting things they said or retelling a story.

The poster's central question is itself informative: why call this deficient rather than a different format of autobiographical memory? The experience contains personal knowledge but not a felt return to the event.

## Structure of the experience

- **Personal semantic content:** life facts remain available.
- **Episodic reliving:** reported absent.
- **Emotional reinstatement:** reported absent.
- **Metacognitive discovery:** the difference was not obvious until explicit comparison with other people.

## Functional consequences

The author reports little obvious day-to-day impairment and questions deficit-based language. A separate commenter in the thread offers a stronger contrast—five clinical bullet points for an entire college career while a spouse can narrate an old work shift for an hour—but that is a separate account.

## Key contrasts

`knowing personal facts` / `re-experiencing the event`  
`present emotion` / `remembered emotion`

## Why this case matters

The account directly operationalizes the survey's distinction between experience and knowledge. It also warns the catalog not to infer functional disability from an unusual representational format.

## Source boundaries

The poster says they are still questioning whether SDAM applies. No formal testing is reported.
