# Speech continuously becomes written subtitles behind the eyes

## Account

The poster reports automatically seeing words whenever they speak or listen, “like living with subtitles on.” The writing appears behind the eyes rather than as ordinary print in external space. At high conversational speed, only the main or stressed words remain visible.

When the speaker uses a language the poster does not understand, stable words fail to form. Recognized speech sounds instead become isolated letters or something resembling a tangled thread. This provides an unusually specific relation between comprehension and the visual concurrent: understood language yields lexical units; unfamiliar sound yields sublexical fragments.

## Circumstances and course

The poster says they learned to read and write at three and a half and also reports mirror-touch synesthesia. They had treated the orthographic stream as ordinary until comparing it with others.

## Structure of the experience

- **Trigger:** one's own speech and other people's speech.
- **Format:** written language, experienced behind the eyes.
- **Selection under load:** fast speech reduces the stream to stressed or important words.
- **Unknown language:** phoneme-like letters and tangled, nonword forms replace subtitles.
- **Automaticity:** the person does not describe choosing to transcribe speech.

## Functional consequences and strategies

Crowded spaces are tiring because words from multiple surrounding speakers appear “like bullets” and compete for attention. A stay in a country whose language they did not understand was experienced as relief: there was less readable material in the concurrent. The poster suspects this contributes to their dislike of loud talkers.

## Key contrasts

`understood speech → words` / `unfamiliar speech → letters or thread`  
`ordinary pace → fuller transcript` / `fast pace → stressed words only`

## Why this case matters

The report adds orthographic form, lexical comprehension, attentional selection, and crowding to the survey's reading/inner-speech categories. It suggests that written-word imagery can be automatic, structured by linguistic processing, and functionally costly.

## Source boundaries

The account uses the label “ticker tape synaesthesia” and reports an association with mirror touch; the registry preserves those claims without independently diagnosing or inferring causation.
