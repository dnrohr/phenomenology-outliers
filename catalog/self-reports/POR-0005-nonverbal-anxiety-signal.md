# Social anxiety arrives as an undifferentiated nonverbal refusal

## Account

The poster reports no spontaneous inner monologue and aphantasia. Deliberate statements can be formed—for example, repeating a reminder—but even those have no auditory quality. In severe social anxiety, the person does not find a train of propositions such as predictions of judgment or embarrassment. The conscious result is a general, forceful impression equivalent to “NO, DON'T.”

The poster has avoided grocery stores for roughly seven years. In therapy, identifying and disputing the assumptions supposedly preceding that avoidance is difficult because the source material is not already present as sentences or even clear endpoints.

## Structure of the experience

- **Spontaneous thought:** not a narrated verbal stream.
- **Deliberate verbalization:** possible, but nonsounding.
- **Anxiety content:** compressed global refusal rather than explicit predictions.
- **Access over time:** possible causes can sometimes be unpacked retrospectively, but an initially plausible verbalization may prove incomplete during discussion.
- **Awareness:** anxiety is sometimes inferred only from avoidance behavior.

## Functional consequences and strategies

Conventional cognitive reframing is harder because there is no ready-made sentence to challenge. The poster is learning to notice avoidance, pause, and deliberately investigate what may be driving it. A responder describes their own nonverbal thought as a net of connected concepts and suggests perturbing parts of the imagined situation to see which connection changes the response.

## Key contrasts

`affective/behavioral directive present` / `explicit proposition absent`  
`deliberate words available` / `sound-like inner voice absent`

## Why this case matters

It adds a form of consciously available but initially unformulated appraisal to the inner-speech and agency domains. It also shows a practical consequence of assuming all anxiety is phenomenally sentence-shaped.

## Source boundaries

The poster self-identifies the relevant traits and reports severe anxiety and therapy, but the entry does not diagnose either the imagery profile or the anxiety disorder.
