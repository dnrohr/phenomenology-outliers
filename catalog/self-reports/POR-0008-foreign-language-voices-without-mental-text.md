# Internal voices pronounce foreign languages the speaker cannot

## Account

The commenter reports many internal voices. Original thoughts use their own inner voice; remembered speech or text uses the source voice, or an invented voice when the real voice is unknown. Some internal voices speak foreign languages with pronunciations the commenter says they cannot themselves produce aloud.

In a second cross-modal contrast, the person has never seen written text mentally, yet frequently sees spaces, objects, artworks, and geometries.

Elsewhere in the same thread the commenter reports nearly constant voices—self-thought, fragments of prior speech or reading, and music with full instruments and voices. The internally heard ensemble can be so complete that isolating and singing one component is frustrating. Effortful visual meditation suppresses the voices, apparently by occupying attention; dreams are mainly visual and less verbal.

## Structure of the experience

- **Voice attribution:** original thought, remembered source, and constructed source voices differ.
- **Pronunciation:** imagined speech may exceed overt articulatory ability.
- **Music:** multi-part internal playback can be richer than controllable vocal extraction.
- **Visual content:** scenes and geometries are available; orthographic images are absent.
- **Attentional competition:** demanding visualization reduces the voice stream.

## Key contrasts

`imagined pronunciation present` / `overt pronunciation unavailable`  
`visual objects and spaces present` / `mental text absent`  
`full musical mixture present` / `voluntary component extraction difficult`

## Why this case matters

The account distinguishes auditory representation from motor production and distinguishes visual imagery by content class. It also suggests competition rather than simple independence between internal modalities.

## Source boundaries

The foreign-language claim does not specify comprehension, language, or testing. Multiple comments by the same username on one page were combined because they clearly elaborate the same self-account.
